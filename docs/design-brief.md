# Chosen approach: The Keepsake Almanac

## Design Movement

Contemporary editorial scrapbook, informed by archival family albums, independent magazine layouts, and letterpress ephemera. The interface values human marks and asymmetric composition over glossy celebration-page conventions.

## Core Principles

1. **Make care tangible.** Photography, paper texture, stitched rules, and clipped-note shapes turn an abstract tribute into a keepsake.
2. **Honor multiplicity.** The language and imagery welcome every kind of mothering and avoid a single idealized family portrait.
3. **Let the page breathe.** Large, unhurried blocks of white space and deliberately off-center elements create reflection rather than spectacle.
4. **Use warmth with restraint.** The mood is bright and generous, never sugary, hyperbolic, or nostalgic to the point of exclusion.

## Color Philosophy

The foundation is **warm paper** rather than stark white, giving the content the feeling of a preserved letter. Ink black creates editorial seriousness, faded leaf green signals renewal, and clay red—used sparingly—acts as the emotional seal. The signature brand color is **Keepsake Red (#D85B45)**: a handmade stamp of recognition that punctuates moments of gratitude.

## Layout Paradigm

The page behaves like a continuously unfolding album spread. A pinned side rail holds the index and a small living dedication; larger content panels shift between wide text columns, overlapping photographs, margin notes, and pull quotes. The composition is intentionally asymmetric and changes rhythm section by section.

## Signature Elements

- A keepsake-red circular seal, subtly imperfect in outline, repeated as a navigation cue and brand mark.
- Hairline stitched rules and date-stamp labels that structure sections without heavy cards.
- Cropped, taped-photo frames with handwritten margin notes, used as quiet interruptions in the editorial flow.

## Interaction Philosophy

The site rewards lingering rather than pushing conversion. Navigation scrolls gently to each passage, paper notes lift a few pixels on hover, and the dedication control invites a private moment without demanding personal information.

## Animation

Content should arrive in small, calm cascades only when it enters the viewport: opacity with a 12px upward translation over 420ms using a strong ease-out. The red seal may make one restrained, 1.5-degree rotation on hover; photo frames rise by 3px with their shadow softening. All nonessential motion is disabled for reduced-motion preferences. No looping animation, bouncing, or rapid visual effects.

## Typography System

Use **DM Serif Display** for essays, pull quotes, and the main hero statement; its gently literary character makes the tribute feel considered. Use **Manrope** for navigation, labels, body copy, and controls; its calm geometry gives the human texture a clear contemporary framework. Headlines use a compact 0.92 line-height; small labels use uppercase, wide tracking, and a smaller weight rather than heavy bold.

## Brand Essence

**For All the Moms is an editorial thank-you to the people who make room for us, every day.**

Personality: **attentive, luminous, grounded.**

## Brand Voice

Headlines are spare and observant; CTAs are invitations rather than commands; microcopy names real acts of care rather than broad superlatives. Avoid generic celebration language.

Example headline: “The hands that make a life feel held.”

Example CTA: “Pause here. Let someone know they are seen.”

## Wordmark & Logo

The logo is a hand-pressed circular seal containing a simplified nesting-line heart formed by three unequal rings. It has no typography inside it, so it remains recognizable as a favicon and a recurring editorial stamp. The wordmark pairs it with a restrained Manrope label set in spaced caps.
